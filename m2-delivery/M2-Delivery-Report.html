2. The decisions this milestone implements

Each of these was agreed in writing during August, and each is now enforced by the software rather than remembered by whoever is working on it.

Four actions, of which only two are judgements

Approve and Decline are recorded permanently. Need more information is a question: it keeps the case open and may be asked more than once. Set aside is a silent pause that stays in the queue, because a case that disappears when paused is a case that gets forgotten. A candidate who is questioned twice and then approved appears in the record as one decision, not three.

Issuing an invitation is deliberately not among the four. It is a consequence of approval, so it appears only after approval and never as a fifth way of judging someone.

Who the Portal may contact, and when

On a referral, a question goes to the referrer, never to the person recommended. Until the Institute approves them they are being discussed, not dealt with, and they may not know the conversation is happening at all. Set aside and Decline send nothing to anybody. The referrer is told once, and only once: when an invitation has been issued. They are never told about a decline.

The invitation link survives being scanned

LinkedIn and Outlook open links to inspect them before a person ever clicks. An invitation consumed on opening would therefore be spent by a machine, and the real recipient would meet a dead link. Opening changes nothing here; only a completed registration spends the invitation. This is demonstrated below, opened three times before use.

The link is shown once

Only a cryptographic hash of the invitation is stored, so there is no page anywhere — including that one, on a refresh — that can display the link again. Losing it is not a problem: issuing a fresh link is one click and retires the old one at the same moment.

One person, one case, one decision

Somebody may apply unprompted while a collaborator independently recommends them. Both submissions are kept, because each carries something the other lacks, and the Portal suggests that they may be the same person. It never joins them by itself. A shared email address is strong evidence and a shared name is weak, but neither is proof, and a wrongly joined pair of people is far harder to repair than an unjoined one.

Names and titles are never abbreviated by the software

First, middle, family and preferred names allow 100 characters each; organisations and departments 200; professional titles 300. They are counted in characters rather than bytes, so a Lithuanian or Chinese name receives exactly the same allowance as an English one. Whatever is accepted is displayed in full, wrapped onto further lines, and never truncated. The evidence below shows a 187-character named chair displayed whole.

How the Portal addresses people

One rule, written once, used by every message and by the Portal itself: the preferred name if one was given, otherwise the first name, otherwise a courteous greeting with no name at all. That last step matters — before registration the Portal may hold only what was typed, and it must never send "Hello ,".

5. Verified by tests

The automated suite — 101 tests, 298 assertions, all passing — locks in the commitments above so that they are checked on every future change rather than remembered:

  • There are exactly four review actions, and only two of them write a judgement.
  • A question keeps a case open, may be asked repeatedly, and leaves one decision behind after approval.
  • A set-aside case sends nothing, decides nothing, and stays in the queue.
  • A question about a referral reaches the referrer; the person recommended is never a contact point during review.
  • Declining and setting aside send nothing to anybody.
  • The referrer is told when an invitation is issued, once, and never on a reissue.
  • Opening an invitation link repeatedly does not consume it; only registration does.
  • Lapsed, withdrawn and already-used links are met courteously.
  • Two submissions converge only when a person says so; a decided case cannot be joined.
  • The database itself refuses a second live invitation for one person, and refuses to email an invitation with no address.
  • The greeting is never empty, and a 194-character named chair survives storage whole.
  • A complete admission runs end to end, from the website form to an admitted collaborator.

Alongside these: code style, static analysis at level 6, a dependency security audit, and every migration proven to roll back as well as apply.

6. Two things worth knowing

The optional courteous reply on declining an application is not built, deliberately. You described it but had not given final confirmation, and building it unasked would mean the Portal writing to somebody you had chosen not to contact. It waits on your decision and is a small addition whenever you want it.

Where an invitation is sent by copied link, the Portal will show it as issued and later as accepted, but never as delivered, because it did not carry it and cannot see what happened. It will not imply a delivery it cannot observe.
