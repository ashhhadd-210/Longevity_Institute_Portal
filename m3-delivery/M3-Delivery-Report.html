3. The decisions this milestone implements

Search first, never scroll

Sixty seven Fields and four hundred Specialties are nothing to a search and unbearable as a list, and that is truest on a phone. Every result is labeled Field or Specialty, because similar wording exists at both levels. Ampersands, hyphens and British or American spellings are normalized on both sides, so nobody fails to find a term because of punctuation they did not copy exactly.

No dead end

If no Field, Specialty or credential fits, the collaborator writes the term they use, their profile saves, and they carry on. The term belongs to them while it waits and is offered to nobody else. The Institute decides later: approve, correct the wording first, decline, or match to an existing term. A decision is final.

Credentials are records, not acronyms

MD is Doctor of Medicine in most of the world and Managing Director in others. Because each credential is its own record, the same letters can appear several times with different meanings, and the meaning shown beside each is how a person picks the one they hold. Correcting the wording of one record corrects every profile using it at once.

Admitted is not the same as visible

A profile joins the directory once it carries a name, a title, at least one Field and something in the collaborator's own words. The bar is recognition, not completeness. A profile that is not listable returns not found rather than forbidden, so guessing at addresses reveals nothing about who is a collaborator.

The promise made at collection holds

City and region are collected, shown to the Institute, and never rendered on any profile or directory page. A test asserts this specifically.

The Institute maintains its own vocabulary

Renaming a term changes every profile that uses it at once. Retiring a term stops offering it to anybody new and leaves it exactly where it is on the profiles that already hold it. Building this exposed a fault in the release process, which would have restored the original wording on every deployment and silently undone any rename. That is fixed, and a test proves a rename survives a release.

7. Verified by tests

193 tests, 606 assertions, all passing, alongside code style, static analysis at level 6, a dependency security audit, and every migration proven to roll back. Among what they lock in:

  • The Registers load as written: 67 Fields, 411 relationships, Public Health Nutrition spanning its three Fields.
  • Selecting a Specialty never assigns a Field, and a Specialty still makes a person findable under its Fields.
  • Narrowing by Field reorders results and never removes any.
  • Punctuation and spelling variants find the term; nonsense finds nothing rather than guessing.
  • A missing term is recorded without blocking anybody, and is nobody else's vocabulary until approved.
  • A fourth credential and a fourth position are refused by the database, not only by the screen.
  • City is collected and never published; a non-listable profile is not found rather than forbidden.
  • A collaborator may narrow visibility and never widen it; nothing is visible on the open web; an undeclared field fails the build.
  • A rename by the Institute survives a release; retiring a term leaves it on existing profiles.
  • An interest is registered once per pair, only where accepted, never in oneself; feedback records its page.
  • Approving a proposal tells the proposer; declining tells nobody automatically.

8. Three things worth knowing

Two items wait on material from the Institute. The founding collaborator spreadsheet is loaded the moment it arrives. The distinguishing lines for the twenty-nine crowded Fields can be entered in the Registers screen as they are written.

The live servers taught three lessons. Exercising the whole path on the real system before delivery, rather than only in tests, found that the release process would have discarded every uploaded photograph on the next deployment, that the web server had no permission to store one, and that every admitted collaborator carried a hidden directory switch left over from before the directory existed, so a complete profile could be told it was listed while its own page answered not found. All three are fixed: uploads now survive releases, the release itself prepares the storage the web server needs, and a collaborator is listed the moment their profile says enough, with the Institute holding a deliberate switch to withhold anybody. Each has a test or a release check behind it.

Six aliases resolve to nothing yet. They point at Pensions, Insurance and Psychometrics Specialties still being reconciled. The loader skips what it cannot resolve rather than inventing a target; they begin working when the Specialty Register catches up.
