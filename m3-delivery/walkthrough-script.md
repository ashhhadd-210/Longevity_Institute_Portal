# M3 Walkthrough Video — Recording Script

**Target:** 7–9 minutes · plain English · calm pace · two people finding each
other, told as a story.

**The spine:** Helena, an actuary, is admitted and builds a profile. The Founder
maintains the Registers and decides a term she proposed. Rafael, a geriatrician,
is admitted, searches for an actuary, finds her, and registers an interest. The
Founder sees it. Everything agreed in September appears somewhere along that path.

The recorded video (`video/M3-Walkthrough.mp4`) follows this script exactly,
with the narration burned in as subtitles. Use this document if you prefer to
re-record it in your own voice.

---

## Before you record

1. Sign in to staging as the founder off camera, so the video never shows a
   two-factor code being typed.
2. Two browser windows, or one window signing in and out: the Founder's side and
   the collaborator's side. Label them in your head as "you" and "them".
3. Have a JPEG portrait ready to upload. Any orientation; the Portal will crop it.
4. Record the browser window only. Never show the terminal, DigitalOcean, the
   repository or the credentials file.

---

## Scene 1 — The Registers (1 min, Founder, `/admin/taxonomy`)

> "This is Milestone 3, and it begins where your own work began: with the
> Registers. Sixty seven Fields, four hundred and ten Specialties and the alias
> set, loaded from the documents you wrote. And now yours to maintain without me."

Search "public". Point at the four neighbours and the descriptor lines.

> "Here are the crowded neighbours. Public Health, Health Policy, Public Policy,
> Public Administration. The line beside each is the one that helps somebody
> choose. Renaming a term changes every profile that uses it at once. Retiring
> one stops offering it, and leaves it exactly where it already is."

## Scene 2 — Helena arrives (30s)

Direct invitation, name only, copy the link. Sign out. Open the link, register.

> "A direct invitation, as in Milestone 2. And this is what is new: her profile
> and the community are one click away."

## Scene 3 — Her profile (1.5 min, `/profile`)

> "Each part saves on its own, because people do this over several sittings and
> often on a phone. And she is not yet in the directory. A profile joins it once
> it carries a name, a title, a Field, and something in her own words."

Upload the photograph.

> "Whatever shape arrives, it is cropped to the centre and held to the same
> portrait rectangle, so the directory reads consistently."

Fill the title in full, the summary, the about, country and city.

> "Country is shown to collaborators. City stays with the Institute. That was the
> promise made when it was asked for, and the screen says so."

Two positions.

> "Up to three current positions, each with its own organisation and department.
> Senior people hold more than one appointment at once."

## Scene 4 — Where her work belongs (1.5 min, `/profile/classification`)

> "The orientation opens by itself the first time and never interrupts again.
> Classify lightly. Describe richly. She is not being asked to describe
> everything she knows."

Type "actuar".

> "Search first, never a list to scroll."

Type "physio" and pause on the result.

> "Type the word you use. Physiotherapy leads to Physical Therapy, and the screen
> quietly says why."

Add a Specialty.

> "A Specialty is optional. Selecting one never assigns her a Field. A journalist
> who writes about health policy is a journalist."

Propose "Longevity Economics".

> "And no dead end. If nothing fits, she writes the term she uses and carries on.
> The Institute decides later."

## Scene 5 — Credentials (1 min)

Type "FRCP".

> "Up to three, chosen from the library rather than typed. The same letters,
> several records: FRCP differs between London, Edinburgh and Glasgow, and the
> meaning beside each is how she picks the one she holds."

Type "public health".

> "And the meaning is searchable as well as the letters. Somebody who does not
> think of themselves as an MPH still finds it."

## Scene 6 — As others see it, and the directory (45s)

> "Credentials after the name. The full title, wrapped rather than cut. City
> nowhere on the page. Links show their label and never their address."

> "The directory. She appears because her profile now says enough. Only Fields
> containing somebody are offered, so a small community does not look deserted."

## Scene 7 — Feedback (20s)

Footer link.

> "From the footer of every page, somewhere to say what she found. Your Alpha
> group are the most valuable readers this Portal will ever have."

## Scene 8 — The Founder decides (1 min, `/admin/vocabulary`)

> "Helena proposed Longevity Economics. It arrives with anything close the
> vocabulary already holds, so mapping is one click and a duplicate is hard to
> approve by accident. You may correct the wording before approving; your wording
> is what every profile then shows, including hers."

Approve.

> "It is now in the vocabulary, on her profile, and she has been told. Had you
> declined, nothing would have been sent. Whether to explain is yours."

Show `/admin/feedback`.

## Scene 9 — Rafael finds Helena (1 min)

Admit Rafael, minimal profile, Field Medicine. Directory, search "actuar".

> "A geriatrician searches for an actuary and finds her. Two people who would not
> otherwise have met."

Open her profile, register an interest.

> "What the first Beta uses instead of messaging. He registers an interest, the
> Institute sees it and passes it on. A record and a note, not a conversation,
> and only because Helena said she was open to approaches."

## Scene 10 — Close (20s, Founder, `/admin/interests`)

> "And here it is on your side. One collaborator would like to work with another.
> Passing it on is yours to do, which at Alpha is exactly right."

> "Say where you belong. Add precision where it helps. Show the community who you
> are."
