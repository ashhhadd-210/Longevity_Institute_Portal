2. Point by point

1. Orientation and Profile Standards Covered differently

What the Portal does
The orientation opens by itself the first time a collaborator reaches "Where your work belongs", records that it was seen, and never opens itself again. It stays available from that screen. Its wording is mine: it makes the same points as yours but not in your sentences.

Why
I wrote it before your language was settled, from the principles in the Field Register.

What changing it involves
Replace the wording with your four sentences, verbatim. Add a short Collaborator Profile Standards notice on the first entry, before the profile, covering accuracy, professional presentation, the photograph, legitimate credentials, and the profile as a selected representation. No checkbox: the Portal records that it was shown, with its version and the date and time, in the same way it records the orientation. The Standards are reachable afterwards from the profile page. Adjustment within M3.

2. Professional Identity Covered differently

What the Portal does
A "Professional title" of up to 300 characters, written by the collaborator, shown beneath the name in the directory and on the profile. It is never generated from a position, and there is no Role taxonomy.

What changing it involves
Rename it Professional Identity everywhere it appears, use your prompt word for word, and add a line under the field saying what does not belong there: credentials, Fields, Specialties, interests, achievements. Adjustment within M3.

3. Credentials Already covered

Up to three, no prefixes, chosen from the controlled library and ordered by the collaborator (profile page, "Credentials after your name"). Search by letters or by full meaning; jurisdiction where it matters; the same letters as several records; near matches shown before a request; a request route that never blocks. The library validates terminology only. Since 27 September each record can also carry the issuing body and a plain language explanation, both optional; see the last point in this section.

4. About Your Work Covered differently

What the Portal does
A "One line about your work" of up to 160 characters, shown in the directory card, and required for readiness if the longer text is empty.

Why
It was designed as a directory line. Your About Your Work is a different thing: a short passage written for people outside the profession.

What changing it involves
Widen it to 500 characters, use your prompt verbatim, make it required for readiness on its own, and move it off the directory card and onto the profile as the first thing under the name and identity. Adjustment within M3.

5. About Covered differently

What the Portal does
"About your work (in your own words)", a long optional text, currently allowed up to 5,000 characters.

What changing it involves
Rename it About, limit it to 2,000 characters, and reword its guidance as broader professional context. Adjustment within M3. Nothing needs to be built.

6. Fields and Specialties Already covered

Fields and Specialties are separate claims on the profile. There is no Primary Field. There is no ceiling on Specialties. A Specialty's relationships to Fields exist in a separate table used only by search, and a test proves that choosing a Specialty assigns no Field. Search first, aliases, punctuation and spelling normalized on both sides, every result labeled. Since 27 September, typing "Longevity" explains that it spans the Institute rather than listing everything longevity related, as the Field Register asks.

7. Missing Fields and Specialties Already covered

The collaborator writes the term, the profile saves, they continue. The term is shown on their own profile page as awaiting review and is offered to nobody else until the Institute approves, maps or declines it (Proposals screen).

8. Current Positions Covered differently

What the Portal does
Up to three, each with title (required), organization (optional) and department (optional). The limit is enforced by the database. Positions are independent of the Professional Identity.

What changing it involves
Add country and start year, both optional. Adjustment within M3.

Your technical question
Yes. If Selected Professional Experience is built (point 9), ending a position becomes one action, "this has ended", which asks for the end year and moves the entry across intact. It is included in the cost of point 9 rather than priced separately.

9. Selected Professional Experience Genuinely new

What it involves
A new profile section: up to ten entries, each with title (required), organization (optional; an independent role needs none), department, country, start year and end year (encouraged, not required), and a description of up to 1,000 characters. Entries at the same organization are grouped under it on the profile rather than listed as unrelated employers. The section vanishes when empty. Includes the "this has ended" action from point 8. Visibility: admitted collaborators, the collaborator may show less.

Why it carries more than its share
All eleven sections in points 9 to 19 share one shape: a selected list with a ceiling, a few required fields, and a short description. That shape is built once, here. Every section after it pays only for its own fields and wording.

10. Education and Professional Development Genuinely new

What it involves
Three areas on one screen. Education, up to five: degree, field of study, institution, school or faculty, department, country, completion year, thesis, additional information. Postdoctoral Training, up to three: type, program, institution, department, country, dates, description. Professional Education and Development, up to five: program or provider, area, completion year, description. Each area vanishes when empty.

The integrity rule
A degree is either completed with a year, or marked in progress with an expected year; the structure allows nothing else, and an in progress degree is always shown as such. The three areas are stored as three kinds so that a program attended can never be presented as a degree or as an appointment.

11. Licenses and Professional Certifications Genuinely new

Up to five: credential name and issuing body (required), jurisdiction, year received, expiration, comment of up to 300 characters. No license number is collected, so none can leak. No Active status is stored or shown; the Portal states what the collaborator said and nothing it cannot verify. Honorary doctorates are directed to Honors by the guidance text.

12. Selected Professional and Intellectual Work Genuinely new

Six optional types, up to five entries each, each type with its own central fields: Books and Major Publications (title, type, authors, publisher, year, description, link); Journal Articles (title, journal, co-authors, year, DOI or link, description; no volume, issue or pages); Reports and Essays (with the seven report kinds you list); Film and Media; Patents and Innovations (with the six kinds, no patent number required); Other Selected Work. An empty type does not appear. Entry order is the collaborator's. This is the largest of the sections because the six types differ in their fields.

13. Current or Recent Research Genuinely new

Up to five: project or topic (required), organization, role, dates, collaborators or partners, description of up to 750 characters, link. Presented without any implication that every collaborator conducts research: it simply is not there for those who do not fill it.

14. Professional and Research Interests Genuinely new

One optional text, 1,000 characters, with your prompt. Small: a field and its placement on the profile.

15. What I Welcome Conversations About Genuinely new

One optional text, 500 characters, with your prompt. Small. "Questions I would like to explore" is not built. Note that the profile already has "Open to approaches", the switch that governs whether somebody may register an interest; this text sits naturally beside it.

16. Honors, Awards and Professional Recognition Genuinely new

Up to five: designation (required), awarding organization, year, description, link. Honorary degrees belong here and the guidance says so.

17. Selected Boards and Advisory Roles Genuinely new

Up to five: organization and role (required), type from Board, Advisory, Scientific Advisory, Committee, Council, Other; dates encouraged; description optional.

18. Selected Professional Associations Genuinely new

Up to five: organization or society (required), membership or designation, years.

19. Languages Genuinely new

What the Portal has
A languages field from Milestone 2 that is free text, not shown, not searchable and without proficiency.

What it involves
A structured list of up to ten languages from a controlled list, each with optional proficiency from your four levels, shown on the profile and searchable in the directory ("collaborators who speak Portuguese"). Counted as new because the searchable, structured part is the whole value.

20. Links Already covered

Up to five, label shown, address never printed, order chosen by the collaborator.

21. Photograph Covered differently

What the Portal does
Upload, center crop to one portrait rectangle, still images only, up to 8 MB. Optional: a profile can be ready without one.

What changing it involves
Make the photograph part of readiness (point 24). Reword the guidance to your standard: an individual professional image, head and shoulders, no logos, avatars, group photographs or organizational graphics, with room for cultural and religious dress. The software continues to enforce only what software can judge. Adjustment within M3.

22. Absence Is Not Deficiency Already covered

Empty sections do not render, on the profile or the directory. There is no completion percentage, strength score, badge, star or per profile count anywhere in the Portal, and I will keep it that way in anything built later. The one notice that exists, "Not yet in the directory", states what readiness needs and disappears the moment it is met; it says nothing about optional sections. Directory counts are off.

23. No logos and no artificial affiliations Already covered

No logos are collected or shown. Organization is optional on a position. The same rule will hold in every column C section.

24. Directory readiness Covered differently

What the Portal does
Ready when it has a name, a professional title, at least one Field, and either the summary or the long text.

What changing it involves
Ready when it has a name, a Professional Identity, About Your Work, at least one Field or a pending Field proposal, a country, and a photograph. The notice becomes "Your profile is ready for Directory Review" with Preview beside it. Adjustment within M3. The Submit button on that notice belongs to point 25.

25. Preview, review and publication Genuinely new, small

What the Portal does
The two halves of the gate exist. On the collaborator's side, readiness. On the Institute's side, a recorded switch per collaborator that withholds a profile from the directory. A ready profile currently becomes visible on its own, and "See it as others will" is on the profile page today.

What is missing
The moment between the halves: the collaborator submits, the profile waits, and the Institute approves or returns it for revision with a note. That is one status on the profile, one Submit action, one short screen for you listing profiles awaiting review with Approve and Return for Revision, a message to the collaborator on each decision, and the audit record. It is not the two step feature quoted earlier, which included re-review of every later edit; here a published profile stays published when edited, which is the simpler sequence you describe.

Cost
Section 4. If you would rather not spend it now, the Portal keeps the automatic behavior and your switch, which already gives you the ability to withhold anyone.

26. Directory presentation Covered differently

What the Portal does
The card shows name, country, professional title, the one line summary, and Field and Specialty badges.

Why
The badges made the search results explain themselves while the profiles were thin.

What changing it involves
Card becomes photograph, name with credentials, Professional Identity, country. Badges and the summary come off the card; they remain on the profile and in search. Adjustment within M3.

27. Search Already covered

Search by name, professional identity, Field, Specialty and country, with the alias and normalization rules. No full text search across profile text exists, and none will be built without your authorization. Directory identifies, profile explains, search discovers: that is how the three screens are divided today.

28. Visibility Already covered

As approved on 29 August and as printed in "Who can see what": four audiences, the Institute sets the ceiling, the collaborator may show less, an undeclared field fails the build, administrative notes and contact details private, country visible, city and region Institute only, nothing on the open web, search results obeying the same rules as the pages. Placement of every column C section is given in section 5 so that nothing is left to be decided later.

29. Feedback and contact Already covered

Page level feedback from the footer, read by you in one place. No Report Concern or Suggestion Box exists or is proposed.

30. Vocabulary administration Already covered

Registers screen: add, rename, reorder, retire Fields, Specialties and aliases; distinguishing lines. The v1.0 Registers are loaded: Fields 67, distinguishing lines 29, Specialties 419 with 434 relationships, aliases 144. "Pensions & Retirement Benefits" existed as a Specialty from v0.1 and is a Field in v1.0; the Specialty is retired, not deleted. The Alias Register's "do not force" list is honored by omission, and a test proves Kinesiology does not surface Exercise Science.

31. Referrals and admission Already covered

Nothing changed. Admission and Directory visibility are separate states; a profile that says too little is not listed however long ago the person was admitted. With point 25, visibility would additionally wait for your approval.

Credential explanatory panel Genuinely new, small

What the Portal does
A credential record carries letters, full name, jurisdiction and, since 27 September, an optional issuing body and a plain language explanation. Approval of a requested credential can supply both; the library can be enriched from a list you send. Nothing is displayed yet beyond letters and full name.

What it involves
A restrained panel on hover for desktop and on tap for a phone, showing only what the record holds and omitting what it does not. Small, because the data question is already answered.

5. Where each new section sits in the visibility architecture

So that nothing is placed later by accident: every section in column C is declared at the ceiling "Admitted collaborators", and the collaborator shows less simply by leaving it empty, which is the same rule as Links and the photograph. Three specifics. Licenses never carry a number, so there is nothing to protect. Languages are searchable by other collaborators, like Fields. The Directory Review note you write when returning a profile is seen by the collaborator and the Institute, and never by anybody else. The printed "Who can see what" table gains a row for each section the day it is built, generated as before from the same declarations the Portal enforces.

6. What Alpha can test today, and what needs column C

With the column B adjustments made and nothing from column C, an Alpha participant can: enter the Portal, read the orientation and the Standards, build a profile with identity, About Your Work, About, up to three positions, credentials, Fields, Specialties, links and a photograph, classify themselves without an artificial taxonomy, propose a missing term, preview the profile, become visible on readiness, find other collaborators, register an interest, and give feedback. That is the whole loop in your point 32, except the explicit submit and approve moment, which is point 25.

What it cannot yet do is represent a long career in its own terms. A participant with decades of work has three positions, one long text and a list of links to say it in. That is where the richer sections matter, and it is why I have priced them so that you can choose the few that carry the most weight for your particular Alpha group. If it is useful, my own view: Selected Professional Experience, Professional and Research Interests, and What I Welcome Conversations About do most of the work of making a rich career legible and an early career look complete, and the rest can follow when funds allow. But that is a judgment about your people, and it is yours.
