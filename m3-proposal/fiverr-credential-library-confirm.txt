Yes, exactly that. You have described it correctly.

When a collaborator reaches the credentials part of their profile they begin typing, and the approved library searches as they type. Typing MPH brings up the approved entry with its full meaning beside it, and they select it. Nothing is typed into their profile by hand. They are choosing an entry from the library rather than writing text, which is the whole point.

They may select up to three for display after their name. If what they hold genuinely is not there, the Request option sits on that same screen. They are never sent elsewhere to look for it.

Three things worth knowing, all of which make the library find more and ask you less.

The search will match the meaning as well as the acronym. Someone who knows they hold a Master of Public Health, but does not think of themselves as an MPH, can type "public health" and find it. People do not always know their own credential as a set of letters, and a search that matched only letters would send them to the request form for something we already have.

The same acronym sometimes means different things. MD is Doctor of Medicine in most of the world and Managing Director in others. FRCP differs between London, Edinburgh, Glasgow and Canada. So those will be separate entries, and the meaning shown beside each one is how a person picks the right one. This is exactly why your instinct to store the full meaning matters. It is not only for display afterwards. It is what makes the choice unambiguous at the moment of choosing.

When somebody does use the Request option, the form will show near matches first. If they are about to request "Masters in Public Health" and the library already holds "Master of Public Health", they will see it before they submit. That spares you approving duplicates of things you have already approved.

One expectation I want to set honestly about the beginning.

A library only grows if it starts somewhere. I will seed it with a solid initial set of widely used international credentials across the disciplines we expect, so that most people find what they need on the first day rather than everyone meeting an empty box. I will send you that starting list for review alongside the discipline structure, since your LinkedIn work has probably shown you more of these than mine has.

Even so, your first collaborators will generate more requests than any group after them, simply because they meet the library when it is at its smallest. That is expected rather than a sign of anything going wrong, and it settles quickly. Every approval you make permanently reduces the work for everyone who follows.

And one payoff worth mentioning, because it answers something you raised earlier.

You said many credentials are instantly recognisable within a profession or country and close to meaningless outside it, which is a real problem for an international and interdisciplinary Institute. Because the Portal will now know what every acronym stands for, it can show that. The letters sit after the name, and anyone unfamiliar with them can see what they mean. Free text would have given you letters that nobody could explain.
