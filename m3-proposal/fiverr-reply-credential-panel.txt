Dear Linas,

Good that you raised this now, because it is exactly the kind of thing that is cheap to anticipate and expensive to retrofit.

The short answer is that the credential architecture already points the right way. A credential in the Portal is a record, not a set of letters, and each record carries the letters, the full name, and the jurisdiction where one applies. That is why FRCP can exist three times for London, Edinburgh and Glasgow without confusion. Two of the four things you list for the panel are therefore already there.

The two that are not are the issuing body and the plain language explanation. Neither exists on the record today. I will add both to the credential record now, empty, as part of M3. That is a small structural change with no screen attached to it, and it means the library can be enriched over time, by you in the administration screen or by me from a list you send, without anything being rebuilt later. It also means a credential approved from a collaborator's request has a place for those details from the day it is approved.

The explanatory interaction itself, the restrained panel on hover for desktop and on tap for a phone, is new capability and I will list it in the third column with the others, costed on its own. It is not large. The expensive part of a feature like this is the data, and the data structure is being handled now.

One design note for when you come to it. The panel should show only what the record holds and simply omit what it does not, so a credential with no explanation yet shows its full name and jurisdiction and nothing else, rather than an empty line saying that a description is missing. That is Absence Is Not Deficiency applied to credentials.
